1 · Seeing is not doing

Data shows what happens when we see a warm season: P(note | weather = warm). A cause asks what happens when we make it warm: P(note | do(weather = warm)). They differ whenever something else drives both, for example place: warm regions have a different style of wine for reasons unrelated to this year's weather. The do-operator means "cut every arrow going into the variable and set it by hand". Do-calculus is the set of rules that says when a quantity with do can be rewritten using only what we can observe.

4 · The rules, one at a time

Back-door criterion. To estimate the effect of X on Y, find a set Z of observed variables that (a) contains no descendant of X and (b) blocks every path that starts with an arrow into X. Then P(y | do(x)) = Σz P(y | x, z) P(z).

Front-door criterion. If X affects Y only through a mediator M that we can measure, and nothing confounds X→M or M→Y beyond X, the effect is identified even if X and Y share a hidden cause.

The three rules of do-calculus (Pearl) say when we can drop an observation, swap an action for an observation, or drop an action, by testing d-separation in a modified graph:

  • Rule 1 (ignore an observation): P(y | do(x), z, w) = P(y | do(x), w) if Y ⟂ Z | X, W in the graph with arrows into X removed.
  • Rule 2 (action = observation): P(y | do(x), do(z), w) = P(y | do(x), z, w) if Y ⟂ Z | X, W with arrows into X and out of Z removed.
  • Rule 3 (ignore an action): P(y | do(x), do(z), w) = P(y | do(x), w) if Y ⟂ Z | X, W with arrows into X and into Z(W) removed, where Z(W) = the Z that are not ancestors of W.

The graph is our assumption, not a finding: the data cannot prove it. What the workbench shows is what follows if the graph is right, and which tests (placebos, independences) could show it is wrong. “More like this, but …” (steer the search)

Instead of rating, you steer: change the wine's description along a dimension and look again. We move the wine's vector in WSET space (e.g. +1.5 spread-units of acidity) and search from the new point. Moves accumulate; reset to start over.

How good are they? (offline evaluation)

Without users we cannot measure “did they like it”. We measure proxies on 3,000 random wines × 10 recommendations: does the list share the grape, country, province? how far apart are the scores? and how varied and how spread over the catalogue are the lists? No technique wins everything.

And with users? Collaborative filtering in miniature

Collaborative filtering ignores what a wine is and uses only who liked it: “people whose ratings look like yours also loved …”. We have no consumer data, so this is a toy with made-up ratings, to show the mechanics.

Glossary

Wines are the 25,000-wine sample of the corpus (same reviews as everywhere else); recommendations describe notes written by critics, not what you will taste. Similar notes do not guarantee similar bottles. Review text is shown only on your machine.

Do they arrange the wines alike? Representational similarity (RSA): rank correlation between all pairwise distances in each embedding and in the WSET vectors. 1 = same geometry.
Do they find the same neighbours? Share of a wine's 10 nearest neighbours that are the same in the embedding and in WSET space.
What facts can be read off each space? A simple linear model predicts grape, country, colour and critic points from the vectors (accuracy or R²).
Does the embedding contain the WSET cues? Linear probes predict each WSET dimension from the embedding (AUC for yes/no cues, R² for scales): how much of our hand-made vector is recoverable?
Is the 2D/3D picture honest? Trustworthiness: the share of neighbours in the picture that are real neighbours in the full space (1 = perfect).
