How a review becomes a WSET-style vector (no models, no randomness)

Gaps are kept honest: appearance is never mentioned, alcohol rarely, and cue levels are project heuristics — not validated by WSET-qualified tasters.

Method

Reviews are normalised and matched against a hand-written WSET-style lexicon (aromas, structure cues, readiness). Unmatched frequent words are mapped to descriptors with MiniLM/BERT embeddings, with a negative class and a leave-one-out calibrated threshold. Everything is deterministic: pinned model revisions, fixed seeds, CPU.

Limits

  • Taxonomy follows the WSET Level 4 SAT (Issue 1.2, Aug 2023): 18 aroma clusters (99 descriptors) in 3 tiers and the SAT scale names. Appearance, condition and alcohol are sparse in reviews; quality is a proxy from critic points; many tertiary words (earthy, leather) also appear in young wines.
  • One-of fields: on the card, hyphen-separated lines allow exactly one value (clarity, intensity, colour, condition, sweetness, acidity, tannin level, alcohol, body, flavour intensity, finish, quality, bottle ageing). Assumption A1 (ours, not WSET's): fruit ripeness (unripe – ripe – dried – cooked) is also one-of per note; the matcher keeps the most specific state and flags conflicts as mixed, and Jev answers it as a single choice.
  • Cue-to-level mappings and nose/palate scoping are project heuristics, not part of the SAT, and are not validated by WSET-qualified tasters.
  • Weather is grid-scale reanalysis at region centroids.
